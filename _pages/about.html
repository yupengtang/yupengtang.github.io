About me

I recently graduated from Georgia Tech with dual M.S. degrees in Computer Science and Computational Science & Engineering. My research is on foundation models, and mostly on how far we can trust them: whether language and vision-language models actually use the evidence in front of them, and why they change their answers. I also spend much of my time on the systems side, building the inference and evaluation infrastructure this kind of research depends on.

I am currently a Research Intern at Stanford's CogT Lab, where I develop methods that make multimodal foundation models more reliable and efficient. Before that, during my M.S., I interned as a Machine Learning Engineer at GMI Cloud, where I optimized inference and serving for a 12B diffusion transformer on H100 GPUs and built video super-resolution and virtual try-on pipelines for production.

I also fix bugs upstream in the open-source libraries I work with. More than 25 of my fixes have landed in projects including Hugging Face Transformers, PEFT, timm, Datasets, and Accelerate, as well as PyTorch TorchRL and TensorDict, Google ADK and gemma.cpp, LinkedIn Liger-Kernel, NumPyro, and Axolotl.
