I recently graduated from Georgia Tech with dual M.S. degrees in Computer Science and Computational Science & Engineering. My work focuses on foundation model reliability, multimodal AI, and ML systems. I study how models reason, use evidence, revise decisions, and behave under uncertainty, while building the systems needed to evaluate, optimize, and deploy them at scale.

My research focuses on large language models, vision-language models, and multimodal agents, particularly evidence-grounded reasoning, model revision, uncertainty, post-training, and reliable multimodal inference. I am interested in understanding why frontier models fail, developing methods that improve their reliability, and designing evaluations that expose behaviors hidden by aggregate benchmarks. I also work on GPU-optimized inference, large-scale evaluation infrastructure, retrieval and serving systems, and other problems that span both modeling and systems. More broadly, I am interested in making capable AI systems more reliable, measurable, and efficient under real-world constraints.

I am currently a Research Intern at Stanford's CogT Lab, where I work on LLM and VLM agents for multimodal reasoning and human-AI collaboration. In industry, I work as a Robotics Engineer at Pudu Robotics, engineering autonomous robotic systems for dynamic real-world environments, with a focus on navigation, human-robot interaction, system integration, and reliability.

I contribute upstream fixes and regression coverage to open-source ML systems including Hugging Face Transformers, timm, and Accelerate, as well as PyTorch TorchRL, Google ADK and gemma.cpp, LinkedIn Liger-Kernel, and Axolotl. Previously, I was a Machine Learning Engineer Intern at GMI Cloud, where I worked on production multimodal generation systems, including inference optimization and serving for a 12B diffusion transformer on H100 GPUs, video super-resolution for text-to-video pipelines, and diffusion-based virtual try-on.
